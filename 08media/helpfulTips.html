Helpful Tips

You know when you're looking at a recipe and it call for something you don't have so you debate if you actually need it or if you can substitute it with something you already have? This page is for helpful tips and tricks like that or when it is better to use the thing it's called for. I will also go over some specialty tools that my family uses that I find quite handy in the kitchen.

One of the main cuisine's where I find it best to have the exact ingredient it calls for is most Asian cuisines. It also can be pretty handy to just have some of those ingredients to use in other recipes. If you are also in the Orem area you will see there are two asian markets, one called The Asian Market and one called First Oriental Market. Both are good markets for different things. If I am looking for snacks, drinks or fish; I will go to the Asian Market. If I am looking for other ingredients I go to the First Oriental Market. Since this is a cooking site we'll put directions to that one.

Substitue or Specific?

Sometimes you want the specific ingredient. Sometimes a substitue works. Here's a table comparing some of them.

Specific Ingredient Potential Substitue Why the Specific might be better
Panko Bread Crumbs Panko is similar to breadcrumbs, but where they differ is that Panko is made specifically from a crustless white bread that is processed into lakes and dried. This gives panko a flakier consistency than regular breadcrumbs. This also makes panko dryer and because of that, panko absorbs less oil. This makes your fried foods lighter and cruncher. So you could use breadcrumbs instead of panko but I would recommend using panko as it will yield better results.
Mirin Rice Wine Mirin is a type of rice wine and a very common ingredient in Japanese cooking. It is similar to sake but has a lower alcohol content and a higher sugar content. Much like Dashi stock it is a staple in many Japanese recipes so if you intend on making more Japanese dishes, it wouldn't hurt to add both of these to your kitchen.
Dashi Stock Fish Stock Dashi stock is a seaweed-based stock used in Japanese cuisine. It typically helps form the base of miso soup and many other Japanese dishes. You can find it at any Asian market or potentially in the international aisle at your grocery store.
Buttermilk milk and a little lemon juice It makes the milk a tad more sour. Buttermilk tends to be a bit closer to flavor you want. The later of the two is also easier to spoil
Cake flour for one cup measure 1 cup flour, replace the last two tablespoons with cornstarch Cake flour has more smooth texture
store bought brown Sugar molases and white sugar It is better to have molases and white sugar and make your own brown sugar becasue then you can control how dark you want the brown sugar to be
cayenne pepper twice the amount of crushed red pepper flakes using cayenne paper has a smoother texture
MSG Salt It is benifical to have MSG on hand instead of just Salt. It adds a more developed flavor
hoisin sause bbq sause BBQ will have a slightly sweeter flavor.

Find a Tool or Figure it Out?

Sometimes a specialized tool makes your life a lot easier. Sometimes you can just use whatever. These are just some suggestions of tools that could be handy to add to your kitchen.

Tool Why use?
Bench Scraper A bench scraper makes cleaning up or working with sticky things way easier. It also makes working with flour a lot easier as you can just sweep it in one go.
Digital Thermometer It makes your life so much easier. You don't have to try and read the little temp. It just shows the temp cleanly
Jar opener It gives you a better grip on the jar making it easier to open
Vintige Pickle Container My mom has one of these. It makes it eaiser and cleaner to snack on pickles. I recomend.
Sifter Yes you can just use a strainer but when you have to sift a lot of things a sifter makes it cleaner to do so.
