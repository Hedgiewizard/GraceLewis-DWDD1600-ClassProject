Katsudon Japanese Pork

Skip to Recipe If You Prefer a Phyiscial Copy

Katsudon Japanese Pork is a favorite dinner of my family. It's relatively easy to prep and tastes really good. My dad has been making this for years, and it is one of the first Asian recipes he started making regularly when he started really trying to branch out into other cultures' dishes.

Required Materials and Ingredients

Panko
Panko is similar to breadcrumbs, but where they differ is that Panko is made specifically from a crustless white bread that is processed into lakes and dried. This gives panko a flakier consistency than regular breadcrumbs. This also makes panko dryer and because of that, panko absorbs less oil. This makes your fried foods lighter and cruncher. So you could use breadcrumbs instead of panko but I would recommend using panko as it will yield better results.
Dashi stock
Dashi stock is a seaweed-based stock used in Japanese cuisine. It typically helps form the base of miso soup and many other Japanese dishes. You can find it at any Asian market or potentially in the international aisle at your grocery store.
Mirin
Mirin is a type of rice wine and a very common ingredient in Japanese cooking. It is similar to sake but has a lower alcohol content and a higher sugar content. Much like Dashi stock it is a staple in many Japanese recipes so if you intend on making more Japanese dishes, it wouldn't hurt to add both of these to your kitchen.
Scallions
Scallions is just a fancy name for green onions. They aren't necessary for this recipe but they do add good flavor.

KATSUDON JAPANESE PORK RECIPE

Ingredients

For the Pork

  • 6 center-cut (boneless pork chops, pounded down to a centimeter thick)
  • salt and pepper
  • flour (for dusting)
  • 3 egg
  • 3 cup panko
  • oil (for frying)

For the Sauce

  • 1.5 cup dashi stock or chicken stock
  • 6 teaspoons sugar
  • 3 tablespoon soy sauce
  • 6 teaspoons Mirin

To Finish

  • 6 large eggs
  • 3 medium onion (thinly sliced)
  • 6 servings steamed white rice
  • 3 scallion (chopped)

Instructions

  1. Start by cooking your rice. We'll prep everything else while it's cooking. If you don't have a specific way of making rice, there are instructions here!
  2. Now is also a good time to cut your onions that we will be using later. Slice the onions into about 0.25-0.5 inch slices. Once you have cut your onions, set them aside. We will use them later.
  3. Next, Prep the Pork. For this recipe, you are going to want to pound your pork slices to make sure they are thin. I recommend putting them in a Ziplock bag or something similar and then pound the pork slices. (It helps decrease the mess and makes it easier.)
  4. Season the pounded pork chops with salt and pepper, and dust with a light, even coating of flour. In one shallow bowl, beat the egg. Put the panko into another shallow bowl.
  5. Add a thin, even layer of oil to a cast iron pan or skillet over medium heat. The oil is ready when you throw a panko breadcrumb into the oil, and it sizzles. Dip the pork into the egg to coat. Transfer the pork to the panko and press it evenly into the meat to get a good coating.
  6. Carefully lay the pork chops in the hot oil and cook for 5-6 minutes on one side, until golden brown. Flip and cook the other side for another 5-6 minutes. Drain on a plate lined with a paper towel.
